How to Approach HLD & LLD

Basic HLD & LLD (Design + Diagrams + Real Examples)

Concept (What & Why)

Design sawal ka framework: Requirements -> Constraints -> High-level diagram -> Components -> Data/Traffic flow -> Scale & HA -> Security -> Observability -> Cost -> Trade-offs. HLD mein 'kya aur kyun', LLD mein 'kaise'.

Definition Deep-Dive

QuestionAnswer
Simple definitionA step-by-step method for answering 'design this system' questions and for planning real projects.
Technical definitionStructured design process: elicit functional/non-functional requirements, define constraints, produce HLD (components, interactions, data flows), refine into LLD, evaluate failure modes and trade-offs, and record decisions.
Why does it exist?Open-ended design needs a repeatable structure.
What happens without it?Random, incomplete designs.
When to useInterviews and architecture work.
When NOT to useJumping to tools first.
Real-world analogyAn architect gathers client needs before drawing a blueprint.

Diagram & Internal Working

1 Clarify: functional + non-functional (SLA 99.9%, RPO/RTO, users, budget)
2 HLD   : boxes and arrows, tech choices with WHY
3 LLD   : subnets/CIDR, SG rules, Dockerfile, YAML, pipeline, alerts, runbooks
4 Review: failure modes (what if AZ dies? DB fails? bad deploy?), cost, security

How it works internally (diagram explained)

Requirements become numbers (RPS, SLO, RPO/RTO). Numbers drive choices (multi-AZ, cache, queue). HLD identifies components and flows; LLD specifies configuration. The failure-mode review tests each component: detection, impact, mitigation, recovery time. Trade-offs make constraints explicit.

Hands-On Lab

Lab Goal

Practise a design answer using the template.

Prerequisites

  • Paper/whiteboard or draw.io
  • Timer (30 minutes)

Step 1

Pick: 'Design a highly available blog platform for 100k daily users'. Write 5 clarifying questions and quantified assumptions

Practise requirement gathering.

Step 2

Draw HLD with numbered request flow and label technology choices with one-line reasons

Practise explaining WHY.

Step 3

Add scaling, HA/DR, security, monitoring, cost; then list 5 failure scenarios and responses

Cover the seniors' checklist.

Step 4

Explain the design aloud in 5 minutes and record it

Fluency for interviews.

Interview Questions

Q1. HLD vs LLD?

HLD: architecture, components, interactions. LLD: detailed module/config/schema level design.

Q2. How do you choose between ECS and EKS?

Team K8s skills, portability, ecosystem needs vs simplicity/ops overhead.

Q3. What non-functional requirements do you ask?

Availability, latency, throughput, RPO/RTO, security/compliance, cost, scalability.

Q4. How to show trade-offs?

State options, criteria, chosen option and why, risks and mitigations.

Q5. How do you handle single point of failure?

Redundancy across AZs, health checks, failover, avoiding shared fate.

Q6. How do you show trade-offs in a design answer?

For each major choice state the alternative, why you chose this option under the given constraints, and what would make you change it.

Why: Senior thinking.

Real-world example: Choose ECS over EKS for a small team, revisit if multi-cloud is needed.
