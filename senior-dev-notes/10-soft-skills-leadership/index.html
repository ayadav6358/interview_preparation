    Soft Skills & Leadership

    📚 7 chapters⏱ ~14–17 hrs total

    The topic that actually turns a strong IC into a Senior/Tech Lead. Writing design docs people can actually review, giving code review feedback that makes a team better instead of resentful, mentoring without doing the junior's job for them, estimating and communicating risk honestly, managing stakeholders who don't speak in tickets, making technical calls under disagreement, and telling your own story clearly in a behavioral interview. None of this is "soft" — it's the difference between being trusted with ambiguous, high-stakes work and not.

    Learning Outcomes

    • Write a one-page RFC that gets a decision in one review cycle instead of three weeks of Slack threads.
    • Give code review feedback that is specific, kind, and actually changes behavior — not just "LGTM" or a wall of nits.
    • Run a mentoring relationship with a clear growth plan instead of ad-hoc "let me know if you're stuck."
    • Produce an estimate with a stated confidence interval and communicate slippage before it becomes a surprise.
    • Translate technical tradeoffs into business language for stakeholders without dumbing them down or lying.
    • Make a reversible decision fast and an irreversible one carefully, and disagree with a manager or peer without it becoming personal.
    • Answer behavioral interview questions with real STAR answers that prove seniority through specifics, not adjectives.

    🏆 Capstone Project: RFC → Review → Behavioral Interview Script

    Problem statement: Produce a complete leadership artifact set for a real system you'd actually like to build (not a toy), then rehearse presenting and defending it exactly as you would in a senior/staff loop.

    Required deliverables:

    • A real one-page RFC/design doc (use the template from Chapter 1) for a system you'd genuinely want to build — pick something with a real tradeoff in it (consistency vs latency, build vs buy, monolith vs service split), not a CRUD app.
    • A self-review of that RFC using the code-review checklist from Chapter 2, written as if a skeptical staff engineer were leaving the comments — find at least 3 real weaknesses and fix them in a v2.
    • A one-paragraph stakeholder summary of the same RFC for a non-technical VP (Chapter 5 translation skill), with no jargon and a clear ask (budget, headcount, timeline, or decision needed).
    • 5 STAR-format behavioral answers (Chapter 7) covering: a conflict with a teammate, a disagreement with a manager, a project that failed, mentoring someone, and a tough tradeoff made under pressure.
    • A full 45-minute mock interview script: 20 min system-design presentation of your RFC with 3 anticipated interviewer pushback questions and your answers, 20 min behavioral Q&A using your STAR answers, 5 min for your own questions to the interviewer.

    Suggested approach: Write the RFC first and let it sit for a day. Come back and self-critique it cold — you'll see the gaps you couldn't see while writing. Record yourself (phone video is fine) delivering the 20-minute design presentation out loud at least once; reading it silently hides pacing and filler-word problems that a real interview will expose.

    Evaluation checklist:

    • ☐ The RFC states a clear recommendation and at least 2 alternatives you rejected, with reasons.
    • ☐ The self-review finds real weaknesses, not cosmetic ones (typos don't count).
    • ☐ The stakeholder summary has zero unexplained acronyms and ends with one explicit ask.
    • ☐ Each STAR answer has a measurable Result (a number, a before/after, a concrete outcome).
    • ☐ You can deliver the 20-minute design pitch out loud, from memory, in under 22 minutes.
